My proposed agenda connects structured reasoning, explicit verification, and resource-aware experimentation.</p><div class="research-grid"><div><h3>Auditable scientific state</h3><p>Represent hypotheses, data provenance, assumptions, experiments, and claims in a typed dependency graph, so agents can identify missing evidence and revisit affected conclusions.</p></div><div><h3>Adaptive verification & recovery</h3><p>Study when to trigger independent checks, counterfactual tests, or human review, and how to recover from failures without repeating every prior step.</p></div><div><h3>Validity under compute constraints</h3><p>Evaluate policies that choose between analysis, pilot experiments, full training, repair, and consultation under matched time and compute budgets.</p></div><div><h3>Reproducible agent evaluation</h3><p>Measure valid outcomes, evidence completeness, failure recovery, and resource cost using controlled ablations, independent holdouts, and injected failures.</p></div></div><div class="agenda"><h3>Proposed evaluation plan</h3><p>I plan to compare fixed workflows, unconstrained orchestration, and evidence-gated adaptive policies under matched models and budgets. Controlled ablations would isolate the effects of structured state, conditional verification, and active experiment selection.</p><p>Evaluation would report valid proposal rate, best valid performance, invalid or gaming proposals, latency and GPU-hours per accepted result, evidence-trace completeness, and recovery after injected failures. Independent holdouts and expert adjudication would test generalization beyond a particular judge or rubric.</p><p>Stress tests would include data leakage, collapsed outputs, broken dependencies, misleading intermediate scores, and repeated failure signatures. Human reviewers would receive concise evidence packages containing code changes, data lineage, diagnostic plots, and failure hypotheses.</p></div>

## Doctoral thesis

**Model Optimization for Federated Learning and Flow-Based Image Restoration**  
Liangyan Li · Ph.D. thesis · McMaster University · 2025

The thesis studies gradient-based client selection for heterogeneous federated learning, MoiréXNet for adaptive restoration, and rectified-flow refinement to balance distortion and perceptual fidelity.
